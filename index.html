What this almost certainly means, and what to do about it

Name servers pointing at an "expiration warning" service is what a registrar does when a domain's renewal has not been paid. The domain was first registered on 8 August 2025, so a one-year registration would have fallen due on 8 August 2026 — and registrars commonly switch a lapsed domain off after a grace period of around five weeks. That lines up with today.

We have not seen the account, so we cannot confirm the cause. But the fix is normally quick: whoever registered the domain — you, or the company currently managing your marketing — pays the renewal, and the site usually comes back within hours.

Two questions worth asking today: who holds the login for this domain, and whose card is it on? If the answer is "the marketing company", it is worth knowing that before deciding anything else about that relationship.

Finding 3 — You Told Us You Could Not Name Your Cost Per Lead
That is not a criticism. It is the most useful thing you said.

You are doing about four jobs a week and want to add up to six more. You are paying a company to help, and you are not getting the results. And when our team asked what a lead or a customer costs you, you did not have the number to hand.

Those numbers should be in front of you every week without you having to ask. A marketing company that is tracking properly can tell you, at any moment, what you spent, how many enquiries came in, how many turned into jobs, and what each job cost you to win.

We could not check your tracking for you today, because the site is offline. So the question to take away is simple: ask your current provider for last month's spend, leads and booked jobs, in writing. If they can produce it quickly, good. If they cannot, that is your answer about why the results are not there.

Finding 5 — Minot Is Hail Country, And Your Biggest Competitor Knows It
The work that comes in waves

Your own Facebook banner lists Storm Damage Restoration as a service — so you already do this work. ABC Seamless of North Dakota mentions hail twelve times on its homepage alone, and has built 167 pages targeting Minot and North Dakota searches. After a storm, the firms with pages built for "hail damage roof Minot" and a form that asks about insurance get the first calls.

Not assessed: how much storm and insurance content your site had before it went offline. Google had indexed a home page, an about page, a services page, a residential and commercial roofing page, a contact page and one Minot location page. That structure is a start, not a storm strategy.

Seven Questions To Ask Your Current Marketing Company
Worth asking this week, whoever you end up working with

None of these are trick questions. A company doing a good job answers all seven quickly and without fuss. Anything they cannot answer is worth noticing.

  • 1. Who owns the domain, and whose card pays for it? It should be registered in your name, with your login.
  • 2. What did we spend last month, and on what? Ad spend and fees, separately.
  • 3. How many leads came in last month — calls and forms, counted separately?
  • 4. How many of those became booked jobs?
  • 5. So what did each lead and each job cost us? That is your cost per lead and cost per acquisition, and it is just number 2 divided by numbers 3 and 4.
  • 6. Do I have my own login to the ad accounts and analytics? Not a report they send you — access you control, that stays with you if you ever leave.
  • 7. What happens to the website, ads and accounts if we part ways? The honest answer is "they are yours, and we hand everything over".

If you want a second opinion on whatever they send back, send it to us and we will read it for you, whether or not we end up working together.
